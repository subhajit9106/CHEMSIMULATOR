A small neural network (3 inputs, two hidden layers of 16 tanh units) is trained from scratch on 1,500 simulated pipe cases with sensor noise added, to predict pressure drop from flow rate, diameter and length. It learns from 80% of the cases and is scored against the clean physics on the 20% it has never seen. A linear regression trained on the same data is the baseline.

Noise is added to the training targets only. Scores use the clean physics values.

Finds the pipe diameter with the lowest annual cost: annualised pipe cost rises with diameter while pumping energy falls. Flow, length and fluid come from the Pipe flow module.

Simulates 200 sequential plant readings of flow and pressure drop, with a fault that begins part-way through. The model predicts the healthy pressure drop for each flow, the residual is turned into a z-score using the first 60 healthy readings, and readings with |z| above 2.5 are flagged. Results are scored against the known fault labels.

Flow, diameter, length and fluid come from the Pipe flow module. Fouling grows linearly from the start reading to the full fault size by reading 200. A blockage jumps to the full size at once. The z-score is based on the relative residual (measured minus predicted, divided by predicted), because the noise scales with the signal.

ChemSimulator is an academic project by Subhajit Majumder. Data Lab: nonlinear least-squares kinetics fitting, an in-browser neural-network surrogate (Adam optimiser) with a linear baseline, Monte Carlo simulation, techno-economic optimisation, and anomaly and fouling detection. Methods: Darcy–Weisbach with Swamee–Jain, LMTD, ideal CSTR and PFR first-order kinetics, and steady-state component balance. For learning and quick estimates, not for final design.
